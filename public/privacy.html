Privacy

Torch & Turn · last updated 19 July 2026

The short version

Torch & Turn has no accounts. We never ask for your name, your email or a password. What you type into a session is deleted automatically 24 hours after that session starts — all of it, permanently. There is no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and the site makes no requests to any third party.

Session ends, code retires, data goes with it.

Who we are

Torch & Turn is run by Appwrights Guild, a small hobby project based in the United Kingdom. For data protection purposes, Appwrights Guild is the data controller. You can reach us at appwrightsguild@proton.me.

What we store

When you create or join a session, we store:

  • The room code for the session, which we generate.
  • The name you type in when you join. This is meant to be a character name, but it is whatever you choose to enter.
  • Whether you are the DM or a player.
  • Game data for that session — initiative order, hit points if you turn them on, and conditions.

If you switch on turn alerts, we also store a push subscription for that device. That is an identifier issued by your browser plus the keys needed to send a notification to it. It does not tell us who you are, but it does identify your device, so we treat it as personal data.

Your browser also keeps a small rejoin token in local storage, so that refreshing the page or locking your phone does not throw you out of the game. It holds a session ID and a participant ID, nothing more. It is not a cookie and it is not used for tracking. You can clear it at any time by leaving the session.

What we don't store

  • No accounts, usernames or passwords.
  • No email addresses.
  • No payment details — the app is free.
  • No analytics, advertising, tracking pixels or profiling.
  • No cookies.

How long we keep it

24 hours. An automated job runs every hour and permanently deletes any session created more than a day ago, along with everything attached to it — participants, combatants, conditions and push subscriptions. There is no archive and no backup copy kept beyond that.

In practice this means a game you ran on Friday night no longer exists by Saturday evening.

Why we're allowed to store it

For running a session, our lawful basis is legitimate interests — you asked us to run a game for you, and we cannot do that without holding the game while it happens.

For turn alerts, our lawful basis is consent. Your browser asks before we can send you anything, and you can withdraw it at any time by turning alerts off in the app or revoking notification permission in your browser settings.

Who else touches it

We use two service providers, both acting on our instructions:

  • Vercel hosts the website.
  • Supabase hosts the database.

Both keep their own server logs, which will include your IP address as a normal part of serving web traffic. We do not use those logs to build any profile of you, and we do not combine them with anything else.

If you turn on turn alerts, the notification itself is delivered through your browser maker's push service — Apple, Google or Mozilla, depending on your device. That is how web notifications work on every site; we cannot send you one without going through them.

We do not sell your data, share it for advertising, or pass it to anyone else.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you have the right to access your data, correct it, have it deleted, object to how it is used, and receive a copy of it.

Being straight with you about a limitation here: because Torch & Turn has no accounts, we hold nothing that identifies you. If you emailed us asking for your data, we would have no way of telling which rows in the database are yours, so we could not reliably act on the request. The flip side is that everything is deleted within 24 hours regardless, without you having to ask.

If you want your data gone sooner than that, leave the session and ask your DM to end it, or email us with the room code and we will delete that session outright.

Children

Torch & Turn is a tool for playing tabletop games and is suitable for all ages. It collects no personal details, so a child using it gives away nothing beyond a character name that vanishes the next day. We would still suggest younger players use a character name rather than their real one — which is good practice at the table anyway.

Changes

If we change what we collect, we will update this page and the date at the top. Given the direction of travel is to collect less rather than more, we do not expect this to change often.

Complaints

If you think we have handled your data badly, please tell us first at appwrightsguild@proton.me and we will try to put it right. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office, the UK's data protection regulator, at ico.org.uk.
